01 · The Challenge

Five tools, five logins, zero cohesion.

The Problem

Teams juggle a different app for every job — analytics here, admin there, settings somewhere else. Context-switching is constant, and nothing looks or behaves the same way twice.

The Objective

Bring the whole workspace under one roof: a single shell where dashboards, sales, admin, config and utilities live side by side with one consistent language.

The Goal

Reduce cognitive load and decision fatigue — so anyone can land, scan, and act in seconds, without relearning the interface each time they switch jobs.

02 · Structure First

Low-fidelity, high-certainty.

Before a single pixel of colour, I mapped the entire shell in greyscale — locking the persistent left rail, the global search header, and the tabbed sub-navigation so every screen would feel like the same product.

03 · The Design System

Why Onest?

A dashboard lives or dies by legibility. Onest is a modern geometric sans-serif that balances clarity with personality.

1

High x-height

Improves readability at small sizes — crucial for dense dashboard data tables.

2

Open apertures

Keeps letters distinct, preventing ambiguity between similar shapes like ‘c’ and ‘o’.

3

Geometric construction

Aligns with the grid-based layout of the UI, creating a sense of order and precision.

Colour isn’t decoration — it’s communication. A tight palette guides behaviour and evokes trust.

Why purple?

Purple bridges the stability of blue and the energy of red. It signifies creativity, wisdom and “future-tech” — without the aggression of pure red or the coldness of pure blue.

The 60-30-10 rule

60% neutral backgrounds & whitespace · 30% secondary cards, borders & text · 10% accent CTAs and key data points (#7B61FF).

05 · The Laws We Follow

Designed to reduce cognitive load.

Beyond aesthetics, the interface strictly follows psychological principles — applied to specific components, not just stated as theory.

Hick’s Law

Simplifying navigation

“The time it takes to make a decision increases with the number and complexity of choices.”

The sidebar groups items into Main vs Admin to reduce scanning load, and can collapse entirely to remove distractions while focusing on content.

Miller’s Law

Chunking information

“The average person can only keep 7 (±2) items in their working memory.”

Key metrics are displayed in groups of four, well within the cognitive limit — letting users assess system health at a single glance, without scrolling.

Jakob’s Law

Mental models

“Users spend most of their time on other sites. They prefer yours to work the same way.”

Search sits top-left/center where users instinctively look; profile & settings stay top-right. Breaking these conventions causes friction, so I didn’t.

Tufte · Data-Ink

Let the data speak

“Remove all non-essential ink.”

Charts strip heavy grid lines and borders. Gradient fills add volume without clutter, and tooltips appear only on hover — progressive disclosure keeps the canvas calm.

06 · The Surfaces

One shell, many jobs.

Sales Overview

Numbers that answer “are we growing?”

A five-metric header (revenue, orders, customers, growth, AOV) sits above trend, product, region and order-status charts — the whole sales story on one scannable canvas.

Admin · Subscription Plans

Dense tables, kept calm.

Plan data lives in a clean table with right-aligned actions and a primary “Add New Plan” CTA — the Data-Ink principle applied so the rows read fast and edit-actions never compete with content.

Admin · System Config

Toggles, grouped by intent.

Messaging APIs, email settings and app config are chunked into labelled cards with consistent toggle affordances — and a single, unmissable “Save Changes” primary action.

Admin · User Management

Scan, don’t read.

Avatars and role badges act as visual anchors for rapid row-scanning (Law of Proximity); name + email sit close together while destructive actions are separated by whitespace.

07 · The Outcome

A system, not a screen.

Binaried isn’t a single dashboard — it’s a repeatable language. Because every surface inherits the same shell, type and colour logic, new modules ship faster and feel native on day one.

1 design system

A single source of truth — Onest type, the purple 60-30-10 palette and shared components drive every screen.

5+ unified surfaces

Dashboard, Sales, Admin (Plans / Config / Users) and Utilities all live in one consistent shell.

4-item metric chunks

Key stats grouped within Miller’s cognitive limit, so status reads at a glance — no scrolling required.

0 relearning cost

Jakob’s-Law conventions mean a user who knows one surface already knows them all.
